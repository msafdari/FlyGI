# What the evidence says about framing

Researched 2026-09-22 because the first drafts led with the negative result, and
that turns out to be the weakest available opening.

## Findings

**1. Positive beats negative — but arousal beats valence.**
Berger & Milkman analysed every New York Times article over three months.
Positive content is more viral than negative content. More precisely, virality
is driven by physiological *arousal*: high-arousal positive (**awe**) and
high-arousal negative (anger, anxiety) both spread, while **low-arousal**
emotions — sadness, deflation, resignation — actively suppress sharing.

A flat "it didn't work" is low-arousal negative. That is the single worst cell in
the matrix. "A complete fly brain is playing an AI benchmark" is awe, which is
the strongest positive driver they measured.

**2. On LinkedIn, admitting failure outperforms success posts — conditionally.**
Failure and vulnerability posts reliably beat polished success stories for
comments and trust. The mechanism is the **Pratfall Effect**: admitting a
mistake increases likability *for people already perceived as competent*. It
does not manufacture competence; it trades on it.

So the ordering is load-bearing. Competence first, admission second. Opening
with the failure spends credibility that has not yet been established.

**3. Curiosity gaps work, but concreteness has an optimum.**
Vague teasing backfires. Concrete, specific numbers outperform coy setups — up
to a point, past which more detail stops helping. Specific figures like "166,700
neurons" and "3.29× worse than random" are the right level.

**4. Awe drives information-seeking.**
Awe correlates with awareness of one's own knowledge gap and with subsequent
information seeking — which is exactly the behaviour a repo link needs.

## The resulting structure

Every draft in this directory follows this order:

1. **Awe** — the scale, and that it is real. 166,700 neurons, 24.5M synapses, 0
   trainable parameters.
2. **A genuine positive finding** — the wiring is provably not random, 3.59× the
   spectral radius of degree-matched rewirings. This is a real discovery and it
   should not be buried under the negative one.
3. **Surprise** — and still no advantage appeared, from it or from any tested
   graph (the tuned positive control also moved no score -- both decision CIs
   span zero -- which bounds the harness, not just the fly). Surprise is an independent driver of sharing.
4. **Pratfall** — the specific things I got wrong, now that competence is
   established. This is where trust is built, not where it is spent.
5. **A portable principle** — "the controls are what make it science." The
   shareable idea, stated so someone can repeat it without the post.

## What changed from the first drafts

| Before | After |
| --- | --- |
| "The fly lost to random." (low-arousal negative lead) | "A complete fly brain played an AI benchmark." (awe lead) |
| 3.59× buried mid-post as a caveat | 3.59× promoted to the first result |
| Failures front-loaded | Failures after competence is established |
| Titles framed as defeat | Titles framed as discovery with a twist |

## What did not change

The numbers, the retraction, the refusal to name other projects, and the refusal
to soften the negative result. Framing is about **order and emphasis**, not about
what is true. A post that buries the negative result is a worse post *and* a
dishonest one — the entire differentiator here is that the controls were run.
