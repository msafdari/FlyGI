Six clicks. A tiny idea borrowed from a fly. 🪰

Scientists mapped the arrangement of a fruit fly’s visual cells. I used that layout to organize what my puzzle-playing program sees. This Reel shows the six actual clicks that cleared level one of an ARC-AGI-3 game in one local run.

With the same map rearranged, the same program needed 199 actions. Another run was 15 versus 255. In the third, the fly-map version cleared in 57; the other did not clear within 600.

Across 25 public games, the overall results were close: 17 versus 16 first-level completions out of 75 runs each. This one puzzle was the standout.

The borrowed ingredient was a visual layout. My program still learned which clicks changed the screen. I’m building FlyGI to explore what else we can borrow from nature.
